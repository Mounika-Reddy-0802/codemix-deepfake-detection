Is the voice on this call real or cloned?

Voice-cloning tools can imitate anyone from a few seconds of audio, and Indian phone scams are spoken in code-mixed Hindi-English. Detectors trained on English datasets collapse on this speech. This system listens to a call as it happens, scores every 4 seconds of the caller's voice, and warns the person being called, never the caller, when the voice looks synthetic.

1 · Listen

Caller audio arrives from a browser call (WebRTC) or a real phone call (Twilio, 8 kHz G.711 mu-law). It is cut into 4 s windows every 2 s, silence is skipped, and each window is level-normalised to −23 dBFS exactly as the training clips were.

2 · Score

A wav2vec2 detector with LoRA adapters, trained on code-mixed speech through a simulated phone line with XTTS and RVC clones, gives each window P(genuine). Scores are smoothed so one odd window cannot flip the verdict.

3 · Warn

Two fake-leaning windows in a row: a warning tone and caution to the receiver. Four: a spoken warning and an SMS. At hang-up: a call summary. The threshold was chosen on held-out development calls for zero false alarms on genuine callers.

Measured results

Every number below is read from committed result files. Equal error rate (EER, lower is better) with its 95% bootstrap interval. Each code-mixed set has a shortcut floor: the EER that eight simple signal statistics already reach on those clips. A result counts only when it is below its floor.

The three systems

S1: trained on English only. S2: S1 plus LoRA adapters trained on code-mixed audio. S3: the whole encoder trained natively on the same code-mixed audio. S2 and S3 use identical training data.

What the demo model does on whole calls

Deployed model: S2, phone-line condition, trained with XTTS and RVC. Threshold chosen on development calls only; these are held-out evaluation speakers, clips joined into 60 s calls.
