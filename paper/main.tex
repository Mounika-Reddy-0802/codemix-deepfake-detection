\title{Recording Domain, Not Code-Mixing: Closing the Generalisation Gap of
Audio Deepfake Detection on Hindi--English Telephone Speech}
% \author{...}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Deepfake detectors report errors below one percent on English benchmarks.
We ask what that means for Hindi--English speech over a telephone line. A
wav2vec~2.0 detector trained on ASVspoof 2019 LA reaches 0.85\% equal error rate on
English but 58.42\% on code-mixed speech over a simulated narrowband channel, and calls
94.8\% of clean genuine Hindi--English speech fake. A
language-controlled test on 183 unseen speakers shows the gap follows the recording
domain, not code-mixing. Low-rank adaptation of 1.13\% of the parameters lowers
channel-matched error to 2.75\%, but this deployed model never beats an informative shortcut
floor of cheap signal statistics on unseen Tortoise clones over the channel. More training speakers
and real codec audio help most: trained on 248 speakers over simulated G.711 and real AMR-NB,
error on new speakers' unseen Tortoise clones falls from 30.8\% to 8.8\% over the line,
beating an informative floor, and from 36.3\% to 6.0\% over AMR-NB, in all five seeds, with
no unseen-tool audio in training. A larger encoder (SSL-AASIST) fine-tuned on the same data
reaches 0.5\% over AMR-NB and 12.9\% on Chatterbox, a third unseen generator, while the
second, IndicF5, stays at 15.8--21.8\%. Spontaneous speech stays mostly rejected. The
detector warns only the callee on live calls. On 30 real mobile calls the deployed model,
failing mostly at the mobile codec, warns most genuine live callers, the final recipe one in
four, catching five of six seen-tool clones but no unseen ones.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code and data availability}
The code, configurations, frozen speaker pools, manifests, generation job tables, per-clip
scores and results files behind every number in this paper are in the project repository,
at the tag \texttt{paper-v1}; each table's source file is named in the LaTeX source. Training used the
seeds 1234, 2025, 7, 42 and 99, and every split is checked mechanically before training
(Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset}). The generated audio is not released, because it is cloned speech
of identifiable people (Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset}); its metadata and checksums are. The
genuine audio is MUCS 2021 and HiACC, available from their publishers, from which the
repository rebuilds the normalised and telephone versions. The English-only detector and two early adapters
are in the repository through Git LFS; the deployed and final-recipe weights are not yet
public.
