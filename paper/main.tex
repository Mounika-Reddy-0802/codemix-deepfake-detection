\title{Recording Domain, Not Code-Mixing: Closing the Generalisation Gap of
Audio Deepfake Detection on Hindi--English Telephone Speech}
% \author{...}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors report error rates below one percent on English
benchmarks. We ask what those numbers mean for the speech most exposed to voice
fraud: Hindi--English code-mixed conversation carried over a telephone line. A
wav2vec~2.0 detector trained on ASVspoof 2019 LA reaches 0.85\% equal error rate on
English and 48.21\% on clean code-mixed speech, rising to 58.42\% over a simulated
narrowband channel, where its AUC falls below chance. The failure is a rejection of
genuine speakers rather than a loss of sensitivity to clones: at a fixed threshold it
rejects 0.37\% of genuine English and 94.8\% of genuine Hindi--English. A
language-controlled test on 183 unseen speakers, holding speaker and recording fixed,
shows this is not caused by code-mixing: genuine English is rejected as often as mixed
speech, and the Code-Mixing Index does not predict the score. Low-rank
adaptation of the frozen encoder, training 1.13\% of the parameters on
speaker-disjoint code-mixed speech and clones generated for this study, reduces
channel-matched error to 2.75\% (3.27\% averaged over five training seeds) while
retaining 2.42\% on clean English. Hearing more voices matters more than any other
change we tried: the same recipe trained on 248 genuine speakers instead of 21 is better
in every one of five seeds on new speakers (0.91\% against 10.75\%), on two unseen
cloning tools (Tortoise 13.59\% against 29.82\%, IndicF5 27.25\% against 35.40\%) and
in false alarms (0.47\% against 3.40\%); fine-tuning the
whole encoder on identical data is better on the tool it saw (0.83\%) and collapses
to chance on English. Every result is compared, by a paired test over speakers, with
a shortcut floor: the error rate eight cheap signal statistics reach when trained on
the same data and scored on the same clips. Against a cloning tool no model was
allowed to see, the adapted system generalises on clean audio (5.44\% against a
29.49\% floor); over the channel the deployed model averages 35.59\% across five
training seeds against a 37.79\% floor and beats it in none, while full fine-tuning on
a single attack family stays below its floor in every seed (19.72\%). A second unseen
generator, IndicF5, is no easier (30.6\%). Randomising
recording cues during training lowers the deployed recipe's unseen-tool error to
26.63\% over five seeds, an exploratory result that does not yet clear the floor. The
detector runs on live calls
and warns the callee, and only the callee, about ten seconds into the speech, using
the windowing and operating point the evaluation reports.
\end{abstract}
